The market picture

One council set against the others in its group, London boroughs or the rest of England: spend per household, the accommodation mix, where it sits in the spread and the questions that raises. No council is named, but none is anonymous either, as each shows its own published figures. A council far from its peers is a question to ask, not a finding.

What the code enforces

  • No names of people or firms in what is drafted or shared. A screen runs over every draft and edit for figures not in the evidence, firm names by their legal-form endings, capitalised words that may be names, email addresses, postcodes and accusing language; another runs over the shared copies and the demo site as they are built, and over every tracked file and commit message when run on a repository, with a private deny list of names kept outside git (for the radar, the parties named in its case sources). Both are nets, not guarantees: a person reads every draft.
  • Outputs are leads and suggestions for a person, never findings or decisions.
  • With four-eyes on, as it is by default, a second person approves each brief and each plan; with it off, one person may approve, and the file says so. An approval counts only while the text still matches the fingerprint the approver saw.
  • Aggregates only, and small flows held back by the display rule. The households, declarations and shipments are synthetic; the real enforcement cases appear by reference, enforcing body, outcome, date and the route tested, with no names; for three of the four tested cases no case number was found.
  • Every figure traces to a published table and a logged release. Inferences in the method notes and the briefs carry [Certain], [Likely] or [Guessing], and the brief screen refuses a claim without one.
